The Post correspondence problem is undecidable: no computable function decides, given a finite list of pairs of strings over the natural numbers, whether some nonempty sequence of indices makes the two concatenations equal. The proof follows Sipser's *Introduction to the Theory of Computation*: the acceptance problem for machines is undecidable by diagonalisation (Theorem 4.11, over mathlib's partial recursive programs), every partial recursive function is simulated by a single-tape Turing machine, and a decision procedure for the Post correspondence problem would decide acceptance through the computation-history reduction (Theorem 5.15). This is the result that the book *Transducers* (M. Bojańczyk) takes as given for the undecidability of equivalence of rational relations.
