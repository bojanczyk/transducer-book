**How the composition proof goes** (following the book, whose proof leaves the details to the reader). The inner for-transducer is put into the form of a single nest of loops with a loop-free body producing at most one letter per iteration (`Transducers.for_nest_form`, already available); a position of the intermediate string is represented by the tuple of positions at which that nest produces the corresponding letter; the outer for-transducer is translated over those tuples by `Transducers.tr`, whose correctness `Transducers.tr_spec` was also already available. Two things were missing and were added here:

* `RequestProject/PartD/ForFree.lean` — the free position variables of a program (`Transducers.ForProg.freePos`) and the transformation `Transducers.closeProg`, which makes a program closed: each free position variable is bound by an extra loop executed only at the first position, and the empty intermediate string is handled by a constant program. This is what lets the translation be applied at the top level, since only a *bound* position variable of the outer program can be represented by a tuple of positions of the inner input. Correspondingly, the hypotheses of `tr_spec` were weakened from all position variables of the translated program to its free ones — which is what its induction actually needs; the statement is otherwise untouched.
* `RequestProject/PartD/ForCompTop.lean` — the assembly. The composed program computes two flags recording whether the input has at least one and at least two letters (`Transducers.lenProg`); on longer inputs it runs the translation, and on inputs of length at most one — where a nest of loops is of no use, since it produces nothing on the empty input — it runs the loop-free simulation `Transducers.shortSim` of the inner transducer in continuation-passing style (`Transducers.cpsFree`), replacing each of its finitely many possible outputs `v` by the constant string the outer transducer produces on `v`. This is the "simple exercise" the book leaves open about the epilogue of the first transducer, together with the short inputs it does not discuss.
