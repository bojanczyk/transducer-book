**Corollary C.2.8 — a discrepancy in the book, documented, and the corrected statement proved.**
The corollary is printed as "if a function is computed by a two-way transducer, then it is regular", but the proof the book gives for it ("two-way transducers can compute all rational functions by Corollary C.2.7, and they can compute map reverse and map duplicate …; finally, they are closed under composition thanks to Theorem C.2.5") establishes the *opposite* inclusion, `regular ⊆ two-way`. The next sentence of the book confirms this: it announces that "the converse inclusion" — that two-way transducers decompose into the prime regular functions — will be proved later, as Theorem C.2.9. So the inclusion as printed is not a corollary of C.2.5 at all; it is the hard half of C.2.9.

Following the convention already used in this project for Definition A.2.7, Lemma A.2.10 and Theorem B.4.6, I kept the printed statement unchanged as `Transducers.twoWay_isRegular`, with a docstring explaining the discrepancy, and left it as `sorry` together with Theorem C.2.9; and I added and **proved** the corrected statement `Transducers.regularFun_isTwoWay` (with the auxiliary `Transducers.isTwoWay_of_isRegularFun`): every regular function is computed by a two-way transducer. Its proof is an induction on the composition tree — Corollary C.2.7 for the rational primes, an explicit three-sweep transducer for map reverse and map duplicate, and Theorem C.2.5 for the composition step. The new file `PartC/TwoWayRegular.lean` supplies the two missing links (closure under pre-composition with a letter-to-letter map, and the two instances of the sweeping transducer). Both results depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`.
