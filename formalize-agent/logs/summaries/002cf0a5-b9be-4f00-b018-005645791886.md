## How

One new file, `RequestProject/Exercises/CycleMean.lean` (382 lines), carries the maximum cycle mean analysis, reusing the loop combinatorics already in the project (`RequestProject/PartB/PathComb.lean`: `Path.loop_of_not_nodup`, `Path.small_loop`). Weight each transition of an nfa with output by the lengths of the strings it reads and writes, and let `p/q` be the largest ratio (output)/(input) over the cycles through a productive state that read a nonempty input and use at most `|Q|` transitions — a maximum over a finite set of pairs of naturals, which is exactly why the limit of the exercises is rational. Then:

* `CycleMean.outputOf_eq_nil_of_cycle_of_input_nil` — a cycle through a productive state that reads nothing writes nothing; this is the only place where `f` being a *function* (not just a rational relation) is used, and without it the mean would be infinite;
* `CycleMean.cycle_bound` — every cycle, however long, satisfies `q·(output) ≤ p·(input)`, by splitting a long cycle into a short one and a strictly shorter one;
* `CycleMean.path_bound` — every path from a reachable to a co-reachable state satisfies `q·(output) ≤ p·(input) + q·K`, removing the cycles one at a time;
* `CycleMean.exists_linear_rate` — the two-sided bound: the upper half from `path_bound` applied to an accepting run, the lower half by iterating a cycle that *attains* the maximum, preceded by a path from an initial state and followed by a path to a final state (this is where reachability and co-reachability of the optimal cycle are needed).

`RationalHasLinearRate` then follows because the supremum defining `maxOutLen f n` is attained over a finite alphabet (`exists_maxOutLen_eq`, added). The analytic half of the exercises, already in the file, is unchanged.
