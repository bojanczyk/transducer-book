## How the argument goes

The easy direction is an induction on the term: each atom is rational under string representation via a machine of `CombMach.lean` (`RatAtomA/B/C.lean`), and the functoriality combinators are `RatComb.lean`. The converse composes a rational term for the string representation, a rational term for the rational string-to-string function in the middle, and a rational term for a one-sided inverse of the representation.

Three points needed work that the regular case did not:

* **The right-to-left flip-flop machines** were previously reduced to the left-to-right ones by string reversal, which is not rational. They are redone in the right-to-left direction (`rat_terms_define_flip_flop_rtl`, `RatMealyFF.lean`): the annotation puts the state that a reset letter produces *before* that letter, with a separator, so after the split every block carries its state as its **last** letter — which is exactly what `unsnoc` and the finite case distinction on the second coordinate (`rtfun_finCasesR`, from left distributivity) read off.
* **The right-to-left reversible machines** need nothing: Exercise `exer:no-reverse-reversible`, already in the project, removes them from the list of primes, and `RatComplete.lean` uses that shortened list.
* **The inverse of the representation of a product** is the one place the regular proof cannot be transcribed, since it pairs two functions of the same input. Instead `RatComb.pairMarkMach` cuts the representation of a pair into the tagged blocks `L⟨a⟩` and `R⟨b⟩`; a split turns them into two blocks, the co-product inverse turns those into `[inl a, inr b]`, and a second split with two `head` operations (Claim `claim:head`) reads the pair off. No coordinate is ever duplicated.

Elsewhere, adjoining units replaces the diagonal in the finite-domain machinery (`RatFinite.lean`) and binary concatenation replaces the prepending the regular case got from reversal (`RatDerived.lean`); Lemma `lem:terms-define-map-reverse-duplicate` is not needed, as the book says.
