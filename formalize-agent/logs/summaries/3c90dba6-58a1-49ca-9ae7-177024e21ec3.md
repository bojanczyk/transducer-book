**How it was proved.** The argument follows the book: reduce continuity to the statement that *pebble automata recognise regular languages*, and prove that by induction on the number of pebbles. The development is spread over nine new files in `RequestProject/PartD/`:

* `PebbleDef.lean` — the book's pebble-transducer syntax and semantics, moved unchanged out of `Statements.lean` so that the constructions can precede the statements.
* `PebbleAut.lean` — pebble *automata* (answer-valued rather than string-valued), their one-step function, the answer of a run, and general facts about it.
* `PebbleProd.lean` — the product of a pebble transducer with a deterministic automaton for the target language, which turns the continuity statement into a statement about pebble automata.
* `PebbleSub.lean` — the sub-machine: what a `(k+1)`-pebble automaton does above its bottom pebble is a run of a `k`-pebble automaton over the input with that gap marked.
* `PebbleAnn.lean` — the *gap data* of a pebble automaton, and the fact (via a bimachine) that annotating every gap of the input with it is a continuous function.
* `PebbleBisim.lean` — a big-step bisimulation principle for two deterministic systems, used twice.
* `PebbleOne.lean` — one-pebble automata recognise regular languages, by collapsing their two levels into a deterministic two-way automaton and invoking Shepherdson's Theorem (already in Part C).
* `PebbleLev1.lean` — the induction: a `(k+1)`-pebble automaton is simulated by a *one-pebble* automaton over the annotated alphabet, giving `Transducers.pebbleAut_answers_isRegular`.
* `PebbleReg.lean` — the assembly into `Transducers.continuous_of_isPebbleTransducer`.
