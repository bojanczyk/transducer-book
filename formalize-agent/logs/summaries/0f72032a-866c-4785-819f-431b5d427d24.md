```
      {w : List HSym | ∃ (H : HTy) (t : HTerm [] H), R.Consistent t ∧ t.repr = w}
```

* `RequestProject/Common/RegLangClosure.lean` — a general-purpose library with nothing about
  transducers in it. Mathlib has Myhill–Nerode and closure of `Language.IsRegular` under
  complement, union and intersection, but not under concatenation or the Kleene star;
  `isRegular_mul` and `isRegular_kstar` supply those, together with singletons, the empty language,
  finite languages, finite unions and a few conveniences. Each is the same computation: the left
  quotient of the compound language is a fixed function of a bounded amount of information about
  the prefix, and Myhill–Nerode bounds the number of left quotients.
* `RequestProject/PartD/HOStr.lean` — the finite alphabet `HSym` and the string representation
  `HTerm.repr`. Two deviations from the book's fifteen letters were needed and are documented in
  the file and in `THEOREMS.md`: the book's alphabet names none of the atomic functions, and the
  formalised syntax builds a list term one entry at a time, so application, `nil` and `cons` get
  prefix letters of their own.
* `RequestProject/PartD/HOParse.lean` — the languages of the induction, the step equation
  `termLang_succ` (which reads a cons-chain as a Kleene star, the one place where a star is needed,
  a list term having the height of its entries plus one), the regularity of every level, and the
  lemma. Two `example`s at the end check that the language is not the empty one.

* `lem:reduction-strategy-lemma`. Its termination item is the majorant argument, and the book's own
  proof of it is incomplete: the head expansion property (`μ` majorises `b[x ↦ u]` implies `μ+1`
  majorises `(λx.b) u`) is taken for granted there, in a marked `\issue`, with the remark that the
  interaction between the parallel strategy and substitution has to be settled first. Settling it
  needs the substitution lemma for parallel reduction and a sequentialisation of application
  spines, after which the two claims below have to be redone for the resulting strategy. Its
  one-step item asks for a for-transducer that performs one parallel step of β-reduction, with
  De Bruijn index arithmetic, and of every rule of Figure `fig:reduction-rules`, on the string
  representation.
* `cor:normalisation-of-terms-with-resource-bounds`, which is an immediate consequence of that
  lemma and unavailable for that reason alone.
* `claim:atomic-functions-majorised` and `claim:every-term-majorised`, which are stated in terms of
  "`μ` majorises `t`" — defined from the strategy and its step count — and the second of which uses
  the head expansion property the book leaves unproved.

I stopped rather than force these: each rests on the strategy of `lem:reduction-strategy-lemma`,
and one of them cannot be proved as the book proves it.
