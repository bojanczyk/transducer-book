All six exercises of `combinators.tex` about natural transformations are formalised and **proved outright** — none rests on a hypothesis, and none contains `sorry`, a new `axiom` or `native_decide`.

**The definition of naturality (new; the project had none).** In `RequestProject/Exercises/CombNatural.lean`:
* `Transducers.Exercises.TyFun` — the syntax of a functor: an expression built from type variables `var i` by the unit type, product, co-product and list constructors of Definition `def:types`;
* `TyFun.app F A` — the type it denotes under a valuation `A : ℕ → Ty` of the variables, and `TyFun.map F g` its action on a family of functions;
* `Transducers.Exercises.IsNatural F G f` — for a family `f : ∀ A : ℕ → Ty, (F.app A).Elt → (G.app A).Elt`, the book's square: `G.map g (f A x) = f B (F.map g x)` for every `A`, `B`, `g`, `x`.

Variables are indexed by `ℕ` and a family by a whole valuation, so one definition serves any arity — which the last two exercises need — instead of a separate notion per number of variables; the one-variable exercises use `var 0`. Since a one-variable family may then in principle depend on the other variables too, the negative and classification statements are if anything slightly stronger than the book's, and the positive ones exhibit families that do not use that freedom. The file's header docstring records this choice, the alternatives, and why, and `EXERCISES.md`/`THEOREMS.md` repeat it.

**The results** (labels as in the book; Lean names in `Transducers.Exercises`):
* `exer:natural-identity` — `natural_id_eq_id`: a natural family `A → A` is the identity at every type, by the author's constant-function argument.
* `exer:head-operation-natural` — `no_natural_head`: no natural transformation `A* → A`; `not_natural_head_of_claim`: in particular the `head` of Claim `claim:head` is not one.
* `exer:natural-list-function` — `uncountably_many_natural_list`: the natural transformations `A* → A*` are not a countable set, witnessed by the families `lenSelect b` (keep or erase the input list according to a Boolean read off its length), one for each `b : ℕ → Bool`. The exercise's further remark that *every* such transformation comes from a choice function `α` is not formalised; this is noted in `EXERCISES.md`.
* `exer:natural-transformations-from-A` — `natural_toList_eq_replicate` (the classification: the argument is repeated as many times as the value at the unit type has entries), `natural_toList_regularTerm` and `natural_toList_regular` (regular at each type, under string representation).
* `exer:natural-transformations-from-regular-terms` — `regTerm_atomics_natural` (file `CombNaturalAtoms.lean`), the thirteen naturality statements for the atomic functions of Definition `def:regular-terms`, group prefix multiplication aside as in the book, stated for the very functions `Transducers.RegTerm.eval` produces.
* `exer:natural-transformations-list-free` — `natural_listFree_regularTerm` and `natural_listFree_regular` (file `CombNaturalFree.lean`): a natural transformation whose domain functor is list-free is definable by a regular term at every valuation, with an arbitrary codomain functor and any number of variables. The proof is the author's "fixed shape with pointers", arranged so that no normal form of the domain is needed: `TyFun.posTy` (the finite type of positions of a variable), `TyFun.gen` (replace each leaf by its position), `TyFun.sel` (read the entry at a position), `TyFun.map_gen` (the two recover the element); naturality then gives `f A x = G.map (fun i q => F.sel i q x) (f E (F.gen x))` at the generic valuation `E`, the right side is definable for each fixed value (`tfun_mapOf`, induction on the codomain), and the value ranges over the finite type `F(E)`, so the dependence on `x` is the existing finite case distinction `tfun_finCases'`. The book's reduction by distributivity to a disjoint union of products is thereby avoided.
